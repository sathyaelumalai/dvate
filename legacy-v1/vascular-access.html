Vascular access at the bedside

Ultrasound-guided placement, everyday care and removal of vascular access devices, performed in your facility by DVATE nurses.

Ultrasound-guided PICC lines

For IV therapy that will run for weeks or longer

A peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) is placed in an upper-arm vein and threaded toward the heart. It carries antibiotics, nutrition, chemotherapy and other therapies that are too long or too harsh for a standard IV.

  • Vein assessment and placement under ultrasound guidance
  • Single and multi-lumen devices, matched to the order
  • Documentation and care instructions for your nursing staff

Midline catheters

For therapy expected to last days to a few weeks

A midline is a longer peripheral catheter placed in the upper arm. It lasts longer than a standard IV and avoids repeat sticks for residents who need a few weeks of compatible therapy.

  • Ultrasound-guided insertion
  • A good fit for many IV antibiotics and hydration orders
  • Fewer restarts for residents with fragile veins

Ultrasound-guided peripheral IVs

For residents with difficult venous access

When your nurses cannot find a vein after a couple of tries, ultrasound shows us the vein that the eye cannot. The resident gets a working IV with fewer attempts.

  • Difficult IV access support
  • Fewer failed attempts and less bruising
  • Same-visit placement in the resident's room

Port access, de-access and maintenance

For residents with implanted ports

We access implanted ports with the correct non-coring needle, de-access them when therapy ends and keep them flushed and maintained between uses.

  • Accessing and de-accessing
  • Routine flushing and maintenance
  • Assessment of port function

De-clotting of vascular access devices

When a line stops flushing or drawing

An occluded catheter does not always need to be replaced. Per physician order, we work to restore patency so the resident keeps the line they already have.

  • Assessment of the cause of occlusion
  • Catheter clearance per physician order
  • Guidance on preventing repeat occlusions

Dressing changes

On schedule, and whenever a dressing is loose or soiled

Clean, intact dressings protect central and midline catheters from infection. DVATE nurses change dressings and securement devices and check the site at every visit.

  • Sterile dressing and securement changes
  • Site assessment and documentation
  • Staff coaching on dressing care

Removal of vascular access devices

When therapy is complete

We remove PICC lines, midlines and other devices safely when the order ends, and document catheter length and condition.

  • PICC and midline removal
  • Catheter integrity check
  • Post-removal site care

Blood draws

For labs that are hard to collect

We draw blood from existing devices or by venipuncture, which helps when residents have difficult veins or need labs drawn alongside another procedure.

  • Draws from lines or by venipuncture
  • Coordinated with other visit procedures
  • Fewer sticks for the resident

Vascular access consultations

Before you place, and when something is not working

Choosing the right device the first time spares residents from repeat procedures. Our nurses review the therapy, its length and the resident's veins and recommend the device that fits.

  • Device selection based on therapy and duration
  • Troubleshooting existing devices
  • Recommendations for your care plan

Common questions

Do you need a physician order?

Yes. Every DVATE procedure is performed under a physician or licensed provider order. Send it with your request, or our team can tell you what the order needs to include.

Can you place a PICC the same day we call?

Call us with the order and we will give you the earliest visit time we can confirm for your location.

Who maintains the line after you leave?

Your nursing staff handle routine care, and we walk them through it at hand-off. DVATE can also come back for dressing changes, de-clotting, troubleshooting and removal.

Do you work with residents who have very difficult veins?

Yes. Difficult access is a large part of our work, and ultrasound guidance is how we approach it.

Need a line placed?

Send the order and resident details. Our clinical team will confirm the device and schedule the visit.
